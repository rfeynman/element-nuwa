# Binary elemental reaction expansion

The standalone `binary-elements-extra.json` contains **114 additional balanced equations**, representing **107 distinct unordered elemental pairs**, of which **106 pairs** were absent from the 111-scenario public library checked during preparation. It includes 149 referenced substances and 27 cited WebElements chemistry URLs. This is a finite source-backed expansion, not an exhaustive reaction predictor and not all 6,903 distinct pairs of 118 elements.

Each included equation was present explicitly on the corresponding WebElements element-chemistry page. Reactant species are exactly two neutral substances, each containing only one element, and the two elements differ. The named product was never generated by valence rules. Molecular forms and phases were retained, including gaseous Br₂/I₂ and Se₈. Existing substances were reused by composition, charge and phase; their identifiers remain compatible with the current library. New elemental forms carry `elementZ`, including alternative phases of the same element.

## Evidence and review

28 HTML source pages are archived in `raw/binary-elements/`; gallium furnished no accepted equations. `manifest.json` gives original URLs and SHA-256 checksums. Every scenario carries its original `sourceEquation` and section `sourceLocator`, in addition to source IDs. Sources are attributed to Mark Winter, the University of Sheffield and WebElements Ltd; publication dates were not stated and are null, rather than invented.

The source contains several transcription errors. `audit-details.json` records 24 coefficient normalizations encountered during extraction; **22 occur in the final extra library**, with two already-covered equations omitted by exact chemical-identity deduplication. These corrections change only integer coefficients while retaining the explicitly asserted reactants, product formula and phase. Examples include missing product coefficients in alkali-halide equations, the source's cobalt oxide equation, and TiN. Every resulting equation independently passes atom and charge conservation. This mathematical check does not itself verify chemistry.

Excluded source entries include barium halides (the author's prose explicitly identifies those as expectations without explicit references), CrF₆/CrF₅, SeF₆ and As₄O₁₀ pending independent identity/phase corroboration. Equations missing complete phases were omitted. Aluminium was inspected online but excluded from this expansion because its equations include apparent O₂(l)/Cl₂(l) phase mistakes; no silent phase corrections were made. No radioactive or superheavy-element products were invented. Water/acid/compound-reactant reactions were outside the scope.

## Conditions and limits

Source contexts were inspected for heating, ignition, controlled product selection and mixed oxide formation. Labels preserve those qualitative limits without quantities, operating temperatures or preparation procedures. Gaseous bromine and iodine entries are explicitly labeled as heated vapor scenarios. Some direct-halogen passages assert a reaction without specifying complete conditions: their labels state that limitation; a `none` activation value means this catalogue has no additional activation control recorded, **not evidence of guaranteed spontaneous bulk reaction at room temperature**. Consumers should show the label and treat these as selected reported scenarios, not a kinetics prediction.

Multiple reported products can share an element pair and even the same coarse UI controls. The standalone catalogue intentionally preserves these outcomes; consumers must let users choose a cited scenario or otherwise avoid silently presenting one as the universal outcome. Oxygen/nitrogen combustion equations isolate a reported product from potentially mixed air-reaction products. Phase/allotrope, surface passivation, reactant ratio and detailed conditions can change the result. Unknown pairs must remain “not modeled”; this does not establish “no reaction.” These are literature-backed educational examples, not teacher-approved experimental instructions.

## Verification

- Independent Python checks: positive integer coefficients; exact atom balance; neutral charge balance; exactly two distinct elemental reactants.
- Current production `validateLibrary` run against the complete standalone library: **zero errors**.
- Existing-library deduplication uses reactant/product composition, charge, phase and stoichiometric coefficients, not only names.
- The extraction/reproduction helper is `raw/binary-elements/build-extra.py`; it is scoped to this extra library and archived evidence. Its deduplication comparison is the existing public library, so rerunning after merging this dataset would intentionally remove already-merged entries.

No assertion of exhaustive coverage or unique outcomes is made. No user-facing public dataset or main builder was modified by this source-expansion task.

## Production disposition

The independent targeted review excluded `binary-selenium-i2-sei4` and `binary-selenium-f2-sef4` from production, including their unused product substances. The archived candidate above is unchanged for provenance. The merger publishes 112 of the 114 candidate equations; 36 accepted records are equation-only because activation conditions are incomplete.
