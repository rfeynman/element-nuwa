# Iron combustion in pure oxygen

Added two form-qualified fine-iron/steel-wool scenarios, preserving the prior general heated-oxidation records. The new cases require explicit Fe and O2 plus oxygen atmosphere and heat or ignition. They report Fe2O3 or Fe3O4 as selected documented endpoints, not an exclusive predicted oxide. No surface-area measurements, gas purity thresholds, rates, quantities or laboratory procedures are supplied.

[UW–Madison](https://demolab.chem.wisc.edu/general-chemistry-demonstrations-topic-page/atoms-and-molecules-reaction-of-iron-and-oxygen/) reports brighter glowing steel wool and many sparks in oxygen. [University of Washington](https://chem.washington.edu/lecture-demos/combustion-steel-wool) contrasts gentle burning in air with vigorous burning in pure oxygen and supplies Fe2O3 stoichiometry. Existing WebElements iron reference supplies both oxide equations. The pure-oxygen examples explicitly assume fine strands; they do not claim any bulk iron object ignites at once.

The flame effect uses a short combustion glow and a new reviewed sparkShower visual flag, rather than a tall gas-flame illustration. The particles represent incandescent burning material. Heat and solid effects remain; no blast or cooling effect is assigned. Typed flag validation, separate atmosphere choices, exact O2 ingredient matching, oxide alternatives and render regressions are checked. Snapshot .7 contains 435 entries (242 modeled, 193 equation-only), 131 sources, and unchanged pair coverage.
