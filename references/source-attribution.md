PubChem does not apply a universal downstream license to every contributor's content. Its [download guidance](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/assay/assaydownload.cgi) asks users to consult provenance and contributor terms. This release uses elemental factual fields rather than contributor narratives or images. No unrestricted license for all PubChem content is claimed.

[NIST's policy](https://www.nist.gov/open/license) distinguishes Standard Reference Data compilations from other employee-produced data. The configuration panel extracts one ground-state configuration fact and derives shell counts. The separate spectra snapshot transcribes factual line wavelengths and level assignments from NIST’s Handbook tables, with Å-to-nm unit conversion and source notation retained. It does not distribute an ASD database or NIST artwork. It acknowledges NIST and describes the transformation. No blanket claim that every NIST database is public domain is made.

Educational scope: 118 elements, 3,383 ground-state isotope records, 118 configurations and a source-checked reaction catalogue. Metastable states, complete reaction prediction, quantitative reaction dynamics and quantitative many-electron density calculations are outside this release. Electron rings are a schematic, not trajectories. An absent half-life never means stable. External teacher/scientist review has not occurred; automated checks and AI source review are recorded separately.

## Binary equations and nuclear notation

112 additional accepted binary equations are factual transcriptions from [WebElements chemistry reference](https://www.webelements.com/), Mark Winter / University of Sheffield / WebElements Ltd, retrieved 20 November 2026 UTC; source edition dates are unavailable. Each record links its exact element chemistry page. Original source pages and artwork are excluded from public assets. Integer coefficient corrections are logged; two selenium product/phase conflicts are excluded. Thirty-six equations have incomplete activation conditions and are displayed without simulation. The catalogue covers 115 distinct binary pairs; the other pair lookups report a data gap.

Nuclear equations are derived from IAEA A, Z, and decay-mode fields using nucleon-number and charge conservation. [IAEA conventions](https://www-nds.iaea.org/relnsd/vcharthtml/guide.html) and [OpenStax nuclear decay](https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/31-4-nuclear-decay-and-conservation-laws) are linked beside the equations. Branch percentages remain source values. Unspecified daughter excitation/gamma cascades are not invented; fission is schematic and ambiguous modes remain explicitly unavailable.

## Neutral-atom spectral lines (local feature, 22 November 2026)

J. E. Sansonetti and W. C. Martin, [NIST Handbook of Basic Atomic Spectroscopic Data](https://www.nist.gov/pml/handbook-basic-atomic-spectroscopic-data/basic-atomic-spectroscopic-data-handbook), neutral persistent-line tables, retrieved 22 November 2026 UTC. The packaged snapshot contains 1,298 assigned wavelengths for Z = 1–99, including ten wavelengths with no assigned state pair. Original line-reference codes, precision, configurations, terms, J values, energy question marks, and per-element table URLs are retained. The individual tables do not supply an evaluation date.

Transformations: Å to nm, HTML superscripts to Unicode, neutral tables selected, and source state pairs mapped to opposite arrows for absorption/emission. Component pairs lacking a wavelength are omitted rather than assigned a neighboring wavelength. Raw HTML is retained privately under `data/staging/spectra/`; the website serves only normalized factual fields. Reference checksums and retrieval dates are in `data/manifests/spectra-manifest.json`. Acknowledgment follows [NIST’s data policy](https://www.nist.gov/open/license); no blanket public-domain claim, provider endorsement or certification is made.

The two visible strips are an original schematic teaching graphic. Equal-width/color marks do not encode measured intensity, linewidth or absorption strength. Absorption requires population in the lower state; configurations are many-electron state labels where supplied. UV/IR lines have no invented visible color. The Handbook selection is not an exhaustive spectrum, and elements above Z = 99 are explicitly outside this reference.
